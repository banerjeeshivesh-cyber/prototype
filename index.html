Prototype limitation: this uses a general-purpose object detector, not a custom-trained waste classifier. It cannot reliably detect watches, chargers, loose cables or many other items because those are not among its supported object classes. Bottles, cups and food items are grouped as potential waste—not confirmed waste—and other recognized objects appear as regular items. “Plastic bag” is now a supported waste label in the category configuration, but this COCO-SSD model does not natively detect plastic bags yet; a bag-specific trained model is still needed before the camera can reliably identify them. The model’s misleading “tie” and “surfboard” predictions are suppressed in this waste-focused prototype; this avoids those wrong labels but does not itself identify a plastic bag. It can recognize some familiar objects (including phones, laptops, keyboards, mice, TVs and remotes) as possible e-waste, but it cannot reliably tell whether an item is discarded, identify every kind of waste, or detect all electronics such as loose circuit boards and cables. The camera now displays only the model's supported e-device candidates instead of presenting unrelated COCO labels as waste classifications. Plastic-waste detection uses a separate browser-side TFLite model trained for broad waste categories. It may detect plastic bottles and other plastic waste, but does not guarantee recognition of every bag or item. Treat alerts as prompts for human review.
